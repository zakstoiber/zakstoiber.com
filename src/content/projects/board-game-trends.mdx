I pulled every ranked game on BoardGameGeek (about 31,000 titles with 30 or more user ratings, as of
August 2026) along with each game's mechanism and category tags, and asked three questions:
do heavier games really rate higher, how has game design changed, and how fast has solo play grown?

## What I found

- **Solo play went from niche to nearly half of new releases.** 8% of games released in 2000–09
  could be played solo. For 2020–25 releases with 100+ ratings it's 45%, and the jump after 2020 ran
  well above the earlier trend. The level depends on the floor (39% at 30+ ratings, 55% at 1,000+),
  because popular games add a solo mode more often, but the rise shows up at every floor.
- **Heavier games rate higher.** Weight and average rating correlate at 0.55, and the median very
  heavy game rates about 1.5 points above the median light game.
- **Heavier games went solo first.** In the 1990s, 56% of very heavy games (weight 4+) supported solo
  play, compared with 6% of light games.
- **Solo-capable games rate higher at every complexity level,** even comparing only games released
  in the same years.
- **Complexity, recency and popularity together explain more than half of the variation in ratings.**
  Weight alone explains 31%; adding release era, number of ratings and genre brings it to 58%
  (17.5K games with 100+ ratings). Heavier, newer and more popular games rate higher, so the
  dashboard ends with a ranking that takes those biases out.

## How to use the dashboard

The ratings floor at the top (30+ to 2,000+ user ratings) applies to every chart. In the complexity
section, filter to a mechanism or category, or drag across the weight chart to narrow the game list.
At the end, switch adjustments for weight, release era, popularity and genre on and off to see which
games still rate well once those biases are taken out.

## Method and data

- **Source:** the BoardGameGeek XML API, pulled in R.
- **Modeling:** DuckDB with SQL for the joins and aggregates. The adjusted rankings come from a
  regression of each game's average rating on its weight, release era, number of ratings and genre.
- **Visualization:** first built in Tableau
  ([earlier version on Tableau Public](https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/zak.stoiber/viz/BoardGameTrendsComplexityRatingsandtheRiseofSoloPlay/UserRatingsxWeight)),
  then rebuilt as this standalone D3 dashboard.
- **Download the data:** [games](/work/board-game-trends/dashboard/data/csv/games.csv),
  [game mechanisms](/work/board-game-trends/dashboard/data/csv/game_mechanics.csv),
  [game categories](/work/board-game-trends/dashboard/data/csv/game_categories.csv).
